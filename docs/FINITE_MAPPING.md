## Recover finite mappings after intermediate overflow

Remap Range and expression inverseLerp, smoothstep and remap evaluated differences and input spans directly as doubles. With finite endpoints -2^1023 and 2^1023, the span overflows to infinity. The midpoint therefore produced a finite normalized zero instead of .5; other combinations produced NaN or infinity despite a representable affine result. Checking only whether the final result is finite cannot detect the incorrect zero. A remap can also have a nonrepresentable intermediate weight while its output is finite: mapping value 1 from [0, Double.MIN_VALUE] to [0, Double.MIN_VALUE] is exactly 1.

A shared package-private FiniteMapping helper retains the original operation order. Inverse mapping returns that result whenever its difference and span are finite. Remapping returns the original result whenever its difference, span, weight, output span, product and final sum are finite. This preserves ordinary raw bits, signed zero, underflow and rounding without generic reassociation or fused arithmetic. Merely obtaining a finite final answer is insufficient when a preceding span has overflowed.

Only an exceptional intermediate from entirely finite operands and distinct input endpoints enters the fallback. Each finite double is represented exactly as integer units of 2^-1074. Inverse mapping constructs the exact difference/span ratio. Remapping constructs the complete affine numerator and denominator before rounding, allowing output-scale cancellation even when the normalized coordinate itself would not fit in a double. BigInteger arithmetic is exact and bounded by finite-double operand sizes; no decimal display conversion, arbitrary precision cutoff or intermediate quotient rounding is used.

The ratio is rounded to binary double using integer quotient/remainder comparison: nearest value, ties to even, including signed underflow, subnormal-to-normal carry, normal significand carry and overflow. An exactly cancelling numerator produces positive zero; a negative nonzero ratio that rounds to zero retains negative zero. Ordinary calculations keep their original signed-zero behavior. A truly unrepresentable output remains nonfinite and reaches the caller's existing policy.
